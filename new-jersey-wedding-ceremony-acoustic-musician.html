Wedding Ceremony Singer & Live Acoustic Musician for New Jersey Weddings

Planning a New Jersey wedding means coordinating a lot of vendors, and live music is often the one couples wrestle with longest — because it has to work for three completely different moments in one day: a quiet ceremony processional, a relaxed cocktail hour, and a reception that needs to build energy without feeling like a cover-band checklist. Sydney Small has fronted a four-piece band around New York City since 2018, and books into New Jersey as a solo acoustic act, a duo, or the full band — same voice, same repertoire, scaled to your ceremony space and your guest count.

Who Should I Hire for Wedding Ceremony Music in New Jersey?

The right hire is someone who has actually run a live ceremony — held a quiet moment during vows, played a processional without rushing it, and then turned the same room into a dance floor a few hours later — not just a singer with a nice demo reel. Sydney's sound draws on powerhouse voices like Amy Winehouse, Adele, and Aretha Franklin, and her sets are built from pop, R&B, soul, jazz, and rock classics: songs your guests already know, not a rigid, generic setlist.

What a New Jersey Wedding Booking Includes

Ceremony — A stripped-down solo or duo format works best for processionals, the vow moment, and recessional, so the music sits close to the room instead of overpowering it.

Cocktail Hour — Live acoustic sets built from the same pop, R&B, soul, and jazz songbook, kept low-key enough for conversation.

Reception — Scale up to the full four-piece band for dancing, or keep it solo/duo for a smaller or more intimate reception space.

Most bookings run 45 to 90 minutes of live singing, and you can split that time across ceremony, cocktail hour, and dancing however your timeline needs it.

Format Options

  • Full band — for larger receptions that need to fill a room and hold a dance floor
  • Duo — a middle ground for mid-size weddings or venues with space or noise restrictions
  • Solo with backing tracks — the right fit for an intimate ceremony, a small guest list, or a tight venue footprint

If your New Jersey venue doesn't have its own PA system, that's part of the planning conversation from the start.
