If you've never booked live music for a wedding before, the process can feel vague — how much input do you get on songs, how far ahead do you need to lock a date, what actually shows up on the day? This guide walks through what to decide, what to have ready, and what happens from your first message through the last song of the night.

What to Decide Before You Reach Out

Before you send a booking request, have rough answers to these:

  • Your date. Sydney Small is now booking Fall 2026 and Early 2027 — the sooner you confirm, the more flexibility you have.
  • Your venue or address. This determines logistics and whether travel outside NYC's five boroughs applies, which is billed separately.
  • Which moments you want live music for — just the reception, or ceremony and cocktail hour too.
  • Format preference — full band, duo, or solo with backing tracks. There's no wrong answer here; it depends on room size, budget, and the feel you want.
  • A rough budget. The booking process is built around this — you're asked directly what you can pay, and the plan gets built around it rather than a fixed package.

What to Have Ready When You Contact Her

The booking request itself only asks for a handful of things: your name, email, event date, venue or address, event type, and your budget, plus a short description of the event. Have those on hand before you start the form so you're not guessing mid-request. If you already know your first dance song or a must-play track for the ceremony, mention it — this is the kind of detail that gets learned and built into the set.

What the First Conversation Covers

Every booking request is read personally, and you get a reply within 48 hours with availability and a plan. That first response typically addresses:

  • Whether the date is open
  • Which format (full band, duo, solo) fits your venue and budget
  • What's included for ceremony, cocktail hour, and reception
  • Whether a PA system is needed for your venue
  • Travel logistics if you're outside NYC's five boroughs

This is also the point where you can ask about repertoire — Sydney Small's sets draw on pop, R&B, soul, jazz, and rock classics, with influences from Amy Winehouse, Adele, and Aretha Franklin.

What Your Estimate Will Include

Because every event is priced on what the room actually needs rather than off a fixed package, your estimate will reflect the specifics you've already shared: format (band, duo, or solo), how many parts of the day you want covered, whether a PA is required, and travel distance if you're outside the five boroughs. How Long Does Booking Take, and What Does That Depend On?

There's no fixed turnaround published beyond the 48-hour reply window for your first inquiry — after that, how quickly things move depends on your date, how far out you're booking, and how fast you confirm format and budget back and forth. Booking further ahead of your date gives you more room to lock in the format and set details you want rather than taking whatever's left.

What to Expect on the Wedding Day

On the day itself, the band (or duo/solo act, depending on what you booked) arrives ready to cover whichever parts of your day were agreed on: a learned song for the processional, vows, and recessional if ceremony is included; live pop during cocktail hour while guests settle in; and a reception set — typically 45 to 90 minutes — built to keep the floor full. Full band, duo, or solo with backing tracks is confirmed ahead of time, so there's no ambiguity about setup on the day.

After the Wedding

There's no formal post-event step described beyond the performance itself — the booking relationship is direct throughout, by phone or email, which means any follow-up (reviews, referrals, future anniversary bookings) happens the same way you booked in the first place: a direct message to Sydney.
